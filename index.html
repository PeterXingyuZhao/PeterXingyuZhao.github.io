Shattered Compositionality: Counterintuitive Learning Dynamics of Transformers for Arithmetic

Xingyu Zhao, Darsh Sharma, Rheeya Uppaal, and Yiqiao Zhong

Transformers can acquire arithmetic subskills in reverse or in parallel relative to standard procedures. We study how training-data correlations shape these learning patterns and how competition between subskills can lead to brittle generalization.

Paper ↗Code ↗Advisor’s blog ↗
My research contributions

I led the experimental design, implementation, and analysis, connecting how transformers learn arithmetic to the statistical structure of their training data.

  • Statistical diagnostics. I analyzed conditional dependencies among addition digits and developed conditional mutual-information diagnostics to measure which relationships model predictions capture during training.
  • Controlled experiments. I designed an ablation that randomized only the thousands-place answer digit. In the reversed-output setting, models failed to learn the correctly labeled lower digits, revealing interdependence between arithmetic subskills.
  • Generalization at scale. I constructed distribution-shift tests for comparison and sorting, trained transformers with up to 100M parameters from scratch, and evaluated 10 pretrained language models on mathematical word problems with added reasoning steps. These experiments exposed how strong accuracy on familiar problems can coexist with brittle generalization.

Cross-Trait Transfer in Subliminal Learning

Xingyu Zhao and Yiqiao Zhong

A teacher model’s preferences can shape a student’s other traits, even through semantically unrelated training outputs. We investigate the structure of this transfer using behavioral measurements, output distributions, and internal representations.

Paper ↗Code ↗
My research contributions

I led the experimental design, implementation, and statistical analysis of how language models transmit preferences through semantically unrelated training outputs.

  • Network structure and inference. I built directed matrices measuring how each teacher preference changes student responses. Group-label permutation tests, with Holm correction for multiple comparisons, found stronger transfer within related groups—such as city regions and color families—in all 30 experimental settings.
  • Geometry and learning dynamics. I developed six diagnostics from output distributions and internal representation geometry, measuring their correlations with observed transfer. Singular-value analysis of color-transfer matrices revealed a progression from broad, shared behavioral shifts to more differentiated patterns during training.
  • Computational breadth. I fine-tuned 7–8B-parameter models across four model families using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA). On Qwen2.5, I also compared learning from sampled outputs with matching the teacher’s probability distribution. These experiments tested how transfer patterns vary across models and training methods.
Ongoing research

Hidden Transmission in Recursive and Multi-Agent Systems

I investigate whether hidden signals can be amplified, potentially exponentially, through recursive training and multi-agent interaction. Building on subliminal learning, current experiments trace trait transmission as models repeatedly learn from their own or one another’s numeric outputs. Planned extensions study agents that exchange and summarize seemingly benign messages: can shared incentives induce herding, or can separately held secrets combine through implicit communication? The goal is to understand how these effects scale with training rounds and agent count, and whether emergent collusion could create safety risks that are difficult to detect in individual messages.
